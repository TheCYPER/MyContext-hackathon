# Outreach formats

## First contact

Match the user's tone and language. A concise first contact normally contains:

- A specific subject and appropriate greeting.
- The user's relevant background and reason for writing.
- One accurately sourced detail about the recipient's work.
- One truthful related project, with its capability boundary.
- A focused question or a bounded collaboration idea, phrased as a hypothesis.
- A clear request and the user's chosen signature.

Use placeholders for any unknown name or signature. Do not claim that the user read a paper, met the recipient, or achieved a result unless evidence supports it. Verify time-sensitive roles, and check that project claims are suitable for disclosure.

## Follow-up

Reference the actual previous interaction, add one useful update or question, and state one next action. Do not manufacture urgency or imply an established relationship.

## Meeting preparation

Summarize the desired outcome, brief background, relevant shareable artifact, prioritized questions, capability gaps, and possible next actions. Adapt detail to the meeting's actual length.

## Post-meeting journal

Use `journal/YYYY/YYYY-MM-DD-<person>-<event>.md`, `type: journal`, `privacy: private`, `status: active`, and a matching `date`. Keep the filename, date, and year consistent.

Record what happened, what each participant said or committed to, the user's interpretation, and the next action with its owner and target date. Do not invent missing commitments or dates.

Draft IDs can use `draft.outreach.<person-slug>.<yyyymmdd>.<kind>`; event IDs can use `journal.<yyyy-mm-dd>.<person-slug>.<event-slug>`. Drafts remain unsent unless the user reports an actual send, which is recorded separately through the context's write policy.
